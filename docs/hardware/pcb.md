# Rev A and Rev B

| | Rev A — perfboard | Rev B — PCB |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier | Stripboard ≈100 × 80 mm, 30 AWG wire | 2-layer KiCad board, same outline |
| FPC sockets | Off-the-shelf ZIF breakouts soldered to the stripboard | Hirose FH12 directly on the board |
| Shift registers | DIP-16 in sockets | SSOP/TSSOP |
| Pull-ups | 9-pin SIP networks | 4 × 0603 arrays |
| Pico | Socketed on 2 × 20 female headers | Same — socketed, replaceable |
| Mounting | M2 holes drilled to match the sled | M2 holes on the **same pattern**, plus holes matching the X240 base-cover standoffs |
| Firmware | identical | identical |
| GPIO map | identical | identical |

Rev B is a **drop-in**: same connector positions, same holes, same firmware. A Rev A build
can be upgraded by swapping the board. That constraint is deliberate — it keeps one set of
docs, one enclosure design and one firmware.

## Rev B is jumpered, not hard-routed — and why

Which of the keyboard FPC's 40 lines are drive, sense, VCC, GND, backlight and power
button is unknown until probing (#28); the ClickPad's pin count until #29. A board that
hard-routes FPC pins to GPIO before that would be wrong on arrival. So **Rev B** gets the
parts that are certain — the ZIF connectors, the 74HC165 chain with its pull-up arrays,
the PS/2 pull-ups, the backlight MOSFET, decoupling, the Pico socket — and brings the
unknowns to headers that sit side by side:

| Header | Carries | Jumper to |
|---|---|---|
| `J_KB` 2 × 20 | keyboard FPC lines 1–40, one per pin | `J_MAT` |
| `J_MAT` 2 × 17 | DRV0–9 (GP0–GP9), SENSE0–19 (chain inputs), BL−, BL+, PWR_BTN, GND | `J_KB` |
| `J_TP` 1 × 12 | ClickPad FPC lines 1–12 | `J_PS2` |
| `J_PS2` 1 × 8 | 3V3, GND, PS2_DATA (GP21, pulled up), PS2_CLK (GP22), fallback DATA/CLK (GP19/20, DNP pull-ups), LED_A, GND | `J_TP` |

After probing, ~30 short jumper wires (or a tiny "personality" board plugged across both
headers) make the assignment. Sense lines 20–23 are pulled up but not headered in this
revision: the documented ThinkPad matrices need ≤ 20 sense lines, and `J_MAT` is already
34 pins. A Rev C hard-routes the measured map and deletes the headers.

## Rev B schematic blocks

1. **Pico module** — 2 × 20 header footprint, USB at the rear edge, `RUN` broken out to a
   tactile reset button, `TP1/TP2/TP3` broken out to a 4-pin USB-C header.
2. **Keyboard FPC** — `FH12-40S-0.5SH(55)`, bottom contact, on the edge facing the deck.
   All 40 lines go to `J_KB`; every pin is probeable there after assembly.
3. **Sense chain** — 3 × 74HC165, `PL`/`CP` bussed, `Q7 → DS` cascade, 100 nF each, three
   4 × 10 kΩ arrays per chip (12 elements per chip; 8 used, spares tied off).
4. **ClickPad FPC** — `FH12-12S-0.5SH(55)` as the placeholder (a 12-pin socket accepts
   narrower cables if you align pin 1; confirm after #29). All lines go to `J_TP`.
5. **PS/2** — 4.7 kΩ pull-ups on DATA (GP21) and CLK (GP22). Second pair on GP19/GP20 with
   unpopulated pull-ups, for the TrackPoint fallback.
6. **Backlight** — 2N7002, 10 kΩ gate, 100 kΩ pull-down, series resistor footprint, jumper
   selecting 3V3 or VBUS for the anode.
7. **Power button / LED** — GP27 with ESD-friendly 100 Ω series; GP28 → 100 Ω → LED header.
8. **Decoupling and bulk** — 10 µF near the Pico's 3V3 pin, 100 nF per IC.

## Rules and what the generated board is

- 2-layer, 100 × 80 mm. Netclass targets 0.20 mm track / 0.15 mm clearance, vias
  0.6 / 0.3 mm; board minimums 0.127 mm (JLCPCB/PCBWay 2-layer capability). The router is
  given that room deliberately — at 0.25 / 0.18 it left FPC lines unrouted.
- Freerouting routes **all 202 connections** (0 unrouted) in about three minutes at 100
  passes. The back-side GND pour is added afterwards with island removal on, so it only
  adds copper; every connection is the router's.
- `kicad-cli pcb drc` on the routed board: no errors, no unconnected items. The only
  warnings are a few dangling vias — router GND vias whose pour-side copper was removed as
  an island; harmless, and a later revision can delete them.
- Mounting: four M2 holes on the sled pattern (4 mm in from each corner) and three M2.5
  holes for the X240 base-cover standoffs at **placeholder** positions (`MEASURE` — issue
  #44 fixes them).

## Ordering

- **JLCPCB / PCBWay**: 2-layer, 1.6 mm FR-4, HASL or ENIG (ENIG recommended for the 0.5 mm
  FPC pads), 5 boards ≈ $2 plus shipping. Allow 1–2 weeks.
- Order the Hirose connectors and the SMD parts from **DigiKey/Mouser** or, cheaper, from
  **LCSC** in the same basket as a JLCPCB order.
- The interactive BOM (`ibom.html`, generated by KiCad's InteractiveHtmlBom plugin) lists
  every reference designator with its LCSC/DigiKey part number.

## How the files are made

The board is **generated**, not drawn: `hardware/pcb/netlist_model.py` holds the parts,
library names and nets; `gen_schematic.py` writes the `.kicad_sch` and `gen_pcb.py` the
`.kicad_pcb` from it, so the two cannot disagree. Routing is done by freerouting from a
Specctra DSN exported by KiCad's Python API, then imported back; DRC, PDF, gerbers and
drill files come from `kicad-cli`. All of that runs in the pinned `kicad/kicad` and
`freerouting` images via `./dev build pcb`. Edit the model, not the KiCad files.

## Deliverables of milestone M7

- `hardware/pcb/x240_pico_rev_b.kicad_pro` + schematic + PCB (generated, committed)
- `out/pcb/schematic.pdf`, gerbers + drill, position file, interactive BOM
- This page updated with the real ordering parameters and a bring-up log.

Assembly with a soldering iron is realistic: the hardest parts are the FH12 connectors
(0.5 mm pitch — flux, drag-solder, magnification) and the SSOP shift registers. Everything
else is 0603 or larger.
